# Project Plan: MSB-Conditioned Generative Image Reconstruction

Your project can be framed as:

> **Reconstructing a full 8-bit image from only its Most Significant Bit (MSB) bit-plane using conditional generative models, with normalizing flows, diffusion, and/or flow matching.**

The key is to **start simple and establish the problem first**, then introduce the generative components one at a time. Don't begin by combining all three models immediately.

---

## 1. Final system you are aiming for

The complete system will eventually look like this:

```text
                         TRANSMITTER
 ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │                                                           │
 │        Original 8-bit image I                             │
 │                 │                                         │
 │                 ▼                                         │
 │          Bit-plane extraction                             │
 │                 │                                         │
 │                 ▼                                         │
 │              MSB B7                                       │
 │                 │                                         │
 │                 ▼                                         │
 │        Binary compression / entropy coding                │
 │                 │                                         │
 └─────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┘
                   │
                   │     VERY SMALL DATA
                   ▼
             Communication
                   │
                   ▼
 ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │                         RECEIVER                          │
 │                                                           │
 │             compressed MSB                                │
 │                   │                                       │
 │                   ▼                                       │
 │             reconstructed B7                              │
 │                   │                                       │
 │                   ▼                                       │
 │       Conditional generative model                        │
 │                                                           │
 │      ┌──────────┬──────────┬───────────┐                  │
 │      │          │          │           │                  │
 │      ▼          ▼          ▼           │                  │
 │    Flow       Diffusion  Flow          │                  │
 │    model                 Matching      │                  │
 │      └──────────┴──────────┴───────────┘                  │
 │                   │                                       │
 │                   ▼                                       │
 │            reconstructed image                            │
 │                   Î                                       │
 └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The mathematical target is:

$$
\boxed{\hat I = G_\theta(B_7,z)}
$$

where:

* \(B_7\) = transmitted MSB plane
* \(z\) = random latent/noise
* \(G_\theta\) = learned generative reconstruction model
* \(\hat I\) = reconstructed 8-bit image.

The random \(z\) is important because **one MSB plane does not uniquely determine the original image**.

## 2. First establish the fundamental information problem

Before touching diffusion or flow matching, prove what information exists in the MSB.

For an 8-bit image:

$$
I =
2^7B_7+
2^6B_6+
2^5B_5+
\cdots+
2^0B_0.
$$

Your encoder keeps only:

$$
B_7.
$$

Therefore:

$$
I=128B_7+R
$$

where

$$
R\in[0,127].
$$

This gives you an extremely useful reformulation:

$$
\boxed{B_7\rightarrow R}
$$

rather than directly predicting all eight bits.

The final reconstruction becomes:

$$
\hat I=128B_7+\hat R.
$$

I recommend using this formulation as one of your principal experiments.

---

## 3. Define the research questions before implementation

Your project should answer several concrete questions.

### RQ1 — Feasibility

> Can a useful full-resolution image be reconstructed from only the MSB plane?

### RQ2 — Generative reconstruction

> Does a generative prior produce substantially better perceptual reconstruction than deterministic regression?

### RQ3 — Generative model comparison

> How do diffusion, normalizing flow, and flow matching behave when conditioned on only the MSB?

### RQ4 — Fidelity vs hallucination

> How much of the reconstructed image corresponds to the original image, and how much is plausible information generated by the prior?

### RQ5 — Rate-quality tradeoff

Later:

$$
B_7
$$

vs.

$$
B_7+B_6
$$

vs.

$$
B_7+B_6+B_5.
$$

### RQ6 — Practical communication

> How much bandwidth can actually be saved after accounting for entropy coding and side information?

That last question is important. **1 bit/pixel is the raw MSB representation, not necessarily the final compressed bitrate.**

## 7. Very important: don't immediately use compression

Initially:

$$
B_7
$$

should be passed **losslessly** to the reconstruction network.

Don't introduce:

* JPEG
* PNG
* entropy coding
* quantization
* channel noise

yet.

Otherwise, if reconstruction fails, you won't know whether the problem is:

```text
MSB information
     OR
compression artifacts
     OR
generative model
```

First answer:

> **Can the original image be reconstructed from a perfect MSB plane?**

Only after that should you add actual communication compression.

## 8. Phase 3 — Establish simple baselines

This phase is extremely important for your eventual paper.

You need to show that your fancy generative architecture actually improves something.

### Baseline 1 — Constant reconstruction

Given \(B_7\):

$$
\hat I =
128B_7 + 63.5.
$$

This is the simplest possible estimate.

---

### Baseline 2 — Pixelwise regression

Train:

```text
B7
 ↓
small CNN
 ↓
grayscale image
```

with MSE.

This tells you how much can be recovered without sophisticated generative modeling.

---

### Baseline 3 — U-Net

```text
B7
 ↓
U-Net
 ↓
I_hat
```

Train with:

$$
L_{MSE}=\|I-\hat I\|_2^2.
$$

This becomes your strong deterministic baseline.

---

### Baseline 4 — Residual prediction

Instead of predicting:

$$
I,
$$

predict:

$$
R=I-128B_7.
$$

Then:

$$
\hat I=128B_7+\hat R.
$$

This may be substantially easier.

## 9. Phase 4 — Treat reconstruction as a conditional distribution

This is the conceptual heart of your project.

You don't really want:

$$
B_7\rightarrow I.
$$

You want:

$$
\boxed{p(I\mid B_7)}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{p(R\mid B_7)}.
$$

Why?

Because multiple images can produce exactly the same MSB plane.

Therefore the problem is inherently **one-to-many**.

A deterministic network gives:

$$
f(B_7)=\text{one prediction}.
$$

A generative model gives:

$$
G(B_7,z)
$$

and therefore potentially:

$$
\{I_1,I_2,I_3,\ldots\}
\sim p(I|B_7).
$$

That is exactly why diffusion/flow models are relevant.

---

## 10. Phase 5 — First generative model: conditional diffusion

Do **not** start with a complicated hybrid.

First implement:

$$
B_7\rightarrow\text{conditional diffusion}\rightarrow I.
$$

A good architecture:

```text
                 B7
                  │
                  ▼
           MSB Encoder
                  │
                  ▼
            conditioning
                  │
                  │
noise ────────────┼─────────► Diffusion U-Net
                  │
                  ▼
              Image
```

The diffusion model receives:

* noisy target image \(x_t\)
* timestep \(t\)
* MSB conditioning \(B_7\).

Train:

$$
\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t,B_7)
$$

or preferably investigate **v-prediction**.

---

## 11. But predict the residual

I recommend testing:

$$
R=I-128B_7.
$$

Then diffusion operates on:

$$
R.
$$

The model learns:

$$
p(R|B_7).
$$

At inference:

$$
z\rightarrow \hat R
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
\hat I=128B_7+\hat R
}
$$

This explicitly preserves the information you know rather than forcing the network to regenerate it.

---

## 12. Phase 6 — Flow matching

Once diffusion works, implement conditional flow matching.

The conceptual architecture becomes:

```text
              B7
              │
              ▼
       conditioning encoder
              │
              ▼
noise z ───► Flow Matching Network
              │
              ▼
         residual R
              │
              ▼
       128 × B7 + R
              │
              ▼
       reconstructed image
```

Train a velocity field:

$$
v_\theta(x_t,t,B_7).
$$

The model learns a transport from noise toward:

$$
p(R|B_7).
$$

Then compare:

### Diffusion

$$
z\rightarrow R
$$

using iterative denoising.

### Flow matching

$$
z\rightarrow R
$$

using ODE integration.

This comparison itself could become an important experimental contribution.

## 13. Phase 7 — Normalizing flow

Now investigate conditional normalizing flow.

The objective is:

$$
z\sim p(z)
$$

and:

$$
R=f_\theta(z;B_7).
$$

Because the transformation is invertible with respect to \(z\):

$$
z=f_\theta^{-1}(R;B_7).
$$

This lets you explicitly model:

$$
p(R|B_7).
$$

The advantage is that you get an explicit likelihood.

That gives you an interesting comparison:

| Model            | Main characteristic              |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------- |
| CNN              | deterministic                    |
| Diffusion        | stochastic generative            |
| Flow matching    | transport-based generative       |
| Normalizing flow | invertible + explicit likelihood |

---

$$

## 18. Don't rely on PSNR alone

This is especially important.

A generative reconstruction can have:

* lower PSNR
* but much better perceptual quality.

For example:

```text
Original
    │
    ├── MSE model → blurry but pixel-close
    │
    └── diffusion → sharp but slightly different details
```

So use at least:

### Fidelity

* PSNR
* SSIM

### Perceptual

* LPIPS
* DISTS, if practical

### Generative quality

* FID
* KID

### Semantic similarity

Potentially CLIP-based similarity for RGB/natural images.

### Efficiency

* bpp
* encoder time
* decoder time
* memory
* number of generative steps


## 22. A particularly interesting extension: spatially adaptive planes

Eventually, you could ask:

> Do we really need the same number of bitplanes everywhere?

For example:

```text
smooth region → B7 only

edge region → B7+B6

complex texture → B7+B6+B5
```

Then your codec could transmit:

```text
MSB globally
+
additional planes only where needed.
```

That could substantially improve rate-distortion performance.

But keep this as a **later extension**, not part of Version 1.

## Milestone 5 — Normalizing flow

**Implementation status:** k=1 128- and 256-pixel patch models trained/evaluated on HPC; patch-size ablation closed with no material quality gain. Preserve as a negative decoder baseline and defer further NF training.

Model the missing residual, not the full image:

$$
B_7\rightarrow p(R|B_7)
$$

with a four-scale conditional Glow-style flow (FrEIA `AllInOneBlock` coupling, learned channel mixing and global affine transforms; four blocks per scale). Each scale squeezes the residual spatially by 2. A learned B7 feature pyramid supplies matching-resolution conditions to each coupling subnet. The residual is uniformly dequantized and logit-transformed before likelihood training; sampling runs the inverse flow and maps back to the valid residual range.

The initial experiment uses 128×128 patches, 40k optimiser steps, batch size 16, DIV2K train/validation, and k=1. FrEIA modules have fixed spatial dimensions, so each patch is modeled as a separate event; adjacent patches do not share residual context. Report this patchwise limitation in results.

The dequantized continuous likelihood is an estimate of discrete residual coding cost, not an achieved lossless bitrate. Exact coded bpp still requires discrete bin probabilities and an entropy coder (Milestone 8).

## 25. Your biggest technical risks

There are several.

### Risk 1 — One MSB may simply be too little information

This is the most fundamental risk.

Your model could produce images that are visually plausible but poorly correlated with the original.

That's not necessarily a failure—but you need to characterize it.

---

### Risk 2 — Hallucination

Diffusion/flow models can create realistic details that **did not exist in the original**.

That's acceptable for perceptual compression, but not for applications requiring fidelity.

You need to clearly define the application.

---

### Risk 3 — Dataset bias

A model trained on faces might reconstruct plausible faces extremely well but fail on arbitrary images.

Therefore eventually test cross-domain.

---

### Risk 4 — Computation

Full-resolution diffusion can be extremely expensive.

Start at:

$$
64\times64
$$

or

$$
128\times128.
$$

Then move to:

$$
256\times256.
$$

Do not start at 1024×1024.

---

### Risk 5 — Hybrid complexity

A model containing:

```text
NF + FM + diffusion
```

may be difficult to train and may not outperform a simpler model.

Your research should allow the conclusion:

> "Flow matching alone provides the best tradeoff."

That is scientifically much better than forcing the hybrid to win.

---

## 26. The research hypothesis I recommend

I would write the central hypothesis as:

> **An MSB bitplane can serve as an extremely low-rate structural representation of an image, while a conditional generative prior can reconstruct the missing lower-order intensity information, enabling an ultra-low-bitrate perceptual image codec.**

Then the second hypothesis:

> **Flow-based generative reconstruction can provide a better quality–complexity tradeoff than conventional diffusion for MSB-conditioned image reconstruction.**

And the third:

> **Progressively transmitting additional MSB planes provides a controllable rate–distortion–perception tradeoff.**

These are testable hypotheses rather than assumptions.
