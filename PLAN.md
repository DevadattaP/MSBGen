# Project Plan: MSB-Conditioned Generative Image Reconstruction

Your project can be framed as:

> **Reconstructing a full 8-bit image from only its Most Significant Bit (MSB) bit-plane using conditional generative models, with normalizing flows, diffusion, and/or flow matching.**

The key is to **start simple and establish the problem first**, then introduce the generative components one at a time. Don't begin by combining all three models immediately.

---

## 1. Final system you are aiming for

The complete system will eventually look like this:

```text
                         TRANSMITTER
 ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │                                                           │
 │        Original 8-bit image I                             │
 │                 │                                         │
 │                 ▼                                         │
 │          Bit-plane extraction                             │
 │                 │                                         │
 │                 ▼                                         │
 │              MSB B7                                       │
 │                 │                                         │
 │                 ▼                                         │
 │        Binary compression / entropy coding                │
 │                 │                                         │
 └─────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┘
                   │
                   │     VERY SMALL DATA
                   ▼
             Communication
                   │
                   ▼
 ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │                         RECEIVER                          │
 │                                                           │
 │             compressed MSB                                │
 │                   │                                       │
 │                   ▼                                       │
 │             reconstructed B7                              │
 │                   │                                       │
 │                   ▼                                       │
 │       Conditional generative model                        │
 │                                                           │
 │      ┌──────────┬──────────┬───────────┐                  │
 │      │          │          │           │                  │
 │      ▼          ▼          ▼           │                  │
 │    Flow       Diffusion  Flow          │                  │
 │    model                 Matching      │                  │
 │      └──────────┴──────────┴───────────┘                  │
 │                   │                                       │
 │                   ▼                                       │
 │            reconstructed image                            │
 │                   Î                                       │
 └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The mathematical target is:

$$
\boxed{\hat I = G_\theta(B_7,z)}
$$

where:

* \(B_7\) = transmitted MSB plane
* \(z\) = random latent/noise
* \(G_\theta\) = learned generative reconstruction model
* \(\hat I\) = reconstructed 8-bit image.

The random \(z\) is important because **one MSB plane does not uniquely determine the original image**.

---

## 2. First establish the fundamental information problem

Before touching diffusion or flow matching, prove what information exists in the MSB.

For an 8-bit image:

$$
I =
2^7B_7+
2^6B_6+
2^5B_5+
\cdots+
2^0B_0.
$$

Your encoder keeps only:

$$
B_7.
$$

Therefore:

$$
I=128B_7+R
$$

where

$$
R\in[0,127].
$$

This gives you an extremely useful reformulation:

$$
\boxed{B_7\rightarrow R}
$$

rather than directly predicting all eight bits.

The final reconstruction becomes:

$$
\hat I=128B_7+\hat R.
$$

I recommend using this formulation as one of your principal experiments.

## 3. Define the research questions before implementation

Your project should answer several concrete questions.

### RQ1 — Feasibility

> Can a useful full-resolution image be reconstructed from only the MSB plane?

### RQ2 — Generative reconstruction

> Does a generative prior produce substantially better perceptual reconstruction than deterministic regression?

### RQ3 — Generative model comparison

> How do diffusion, normalizing flow, and flow matching behave when conditioned on only the MSB?

### RQ4 — Fidelity vs hallucination

> How much of the reconstructed image corresponds to the original image, and how much is plausible information generated by the prior?

### RQ5 — Rate-quality tradeoff

Later:

$$
B_7
$$

vs.

$$
B_7+B_6
$$

vs.

$$
B_7+B_6+B_5.
$$

### RQ6 — Practical communication

> How much bandwidth can actually be saved after accounting for entropy coding and side information?

That last question is important. **1 bit/pixel is the raw MSB representation, not necessarily the final compressed bitrate.**

## 9. Phase 4 — Treat reconstruction as a conditional distribution

This is the conceptual heart of your project.

You don't really want:

$$
B_7\rightarrow I.
$$

You want:

$$
\boxed{p(I\mid B_7)}
$$

or:

$$
\boxed{p(R\mid B_7)}.
$$

Why?

Because multiple images can produce exactly the same MSB plane.

Therefore the problem is inherently **one-to-many**.

A deterministic network gives:

$$
f(B_7)=\text{one prediction}.
$$

A generative model gives:

$$
G(B_7,z)
$$

and therefore potentially:

$$
\{I_1,I_2,I_3,\ldots\}
\sim p(I|B_7).
$$

That is exactly why diffusion/flow models are relevant.

---

## 10. Phase 5 — First generative model: conditional diffusion

Do **not** start with a complicated hybrid.

First implement:

$$
B_7\rightarrow\text{conditional diffusion}\rightarrow I.
$$

A good architecture:

```text
                 B7
                  │
                  ▼
           MSB Encoder
                  │
                  ▼
            conditioning
                  │
                  │
noise ────────────┼─────────► Diffusion U-Net
                  │
                  ▼
              Image
```

The diffusion model receives:

* noisy target image \(x_t\)
* timestep \(t\)
* MSB conditioning \(B_7\).

Train:

$$
\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t,B_7)
$$

or preferably investigate **v-prediction**.

---

## 11. But predict the residual

I recommend testing:

$$
R=I-128B_7.
$$

Then diffusion operates on:

$$
R.
$$

The model learns:

$$
p(R|B_7).
$$

At inference:

$$
z\rightarrow \hat R
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
\hat I=128B_7+\hat R
}
$$

This explicitly preserves the information you know rather than forcing the network to regenerate it.

```text

              ▼

              │
              ▼
       reconstructed image
```

Train a velocity field:

$$
v_\theta(x_t,t,B_7).
$$

The model learns a transport from noise toward:

$$
p(R|B_7).
$$

Then compare:

### Diffusion

$$
z\rightarrow R
$$

using iterative denoising.

### Flow matching

$$
z\rightarrow R
$$

using ODE integration.

This comparison itself could become an important experimental contribution.

## 13. Phase 7 — Normalizing flow

Now investigate conditional normalizing flow.

The objective is:

$$
z\sim p(z)
$$

and:

$$
R=f_\theta(z;B_7).
$$

Because the transformation is invertible with respect to \(z\):

$$
z=f_\theta^{-1}(R;B_7).
$$

This lets you explicitly model:

$$
p(R|B_7).
$$

The advantage is that you get an explicit likelihood.

That gives you an interesting comparison:

| Model            | Main characteristic              |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------- |
| CNN              | deterministic                    |
| Diffusion        | stochastic generative            |
| Flow matching    | transport-based generative       |
| Normalizing flow | invertible + explicit likelihood |

## 18. Don't rely on PSNR alone

This is especially important.

A generative reconstruction can have:

* lower PSNR
* but much better perceptual quality.

For example:

```text
Original
    │
    ├── MSE model → blurry but pixel-close
    │
    └── diffusion → sharp but slightly different details
```

So use at least:

### Fidelity

* PSNR
* SSIM

### Perceptual

* LPIPS
* DISTS, if practical

### Generative quality

* FID
* KID

### Semantic similarity

Potentially CLIP-based similarity for RGB/natural images.

### Efficiency

* bpp
* encoder time
* decoder time
* memory
* number of generative steps

## Milestone 5 — Normalizing flow

**Implementation status:** k=1 128- and 256-pixel patch models trained/evaluated on HPC; patch-size ablation closed with no material quality gain. Preserve as a negative decoder baseline and defer further NF training.

Model the missing residual, not the full image:

$$
B_7\rightarrow p(R|B_7)
$$

with a four-scale conditional Glow-style flow (FrEIA `AllInOneBlock` coupling, learned channel mixing and global affine transforms; four blocks per scale). Each scale squeezes the residual spatially by 2. A learned B7 feature pyramid supplies matching-resolution conditions to each coupling subnet. The residual is uniformly dequantized and logit-transformed before likelihood training; sampling runs the inverse flow and maps back to the valid residual range.

The initial experiment uses 128×128 patches, 40k optimiser steps, batch size 16, DIV2K train/validation, and k=1. FrEIA modules have fixed spatial dimensions, so each patch is modeled as a separate event; adjacent patches do not share residual context. Report this patchwise limitation in results.

The dequantized continuous likelihood is an estimate of discrete residual coding cost, not an achieved lossless bitrate. Exact coded bpp still requires discrete bin probabilities and an entropy coder (Milestone 8).

### First-run results (Kodak, k=1)

The 40k-step run completed normally. Its best checkpoint was at step 40k with validation dequantized NLL 4.2647 bits/residual-dimension; validation PSNR was 16.21 dB, 1.17 dB below midpoint fill (17.38 dB on the validation crops). The validation PSNR curve rose slowly and flattened near 16.2 dB, while the training NLL continued to fall, indicating a modest train/validation gap but no run failure.

| Method | PSNR ↑ | SSIM ↑ | LPIPS ↓ | DISTS ↓ | KID × 10³ ↓ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Midpoint fill | 17.01 | 0.579 | 0.517 | 0.364 | 81.8 |
| Residual U-Net | 18.60 | 0.637 | 0.455 | 0.293 | 79.8 |
| Diffusion, 3 steps | 17.75 | 0.610 | 0.424 | 0.257 | 54.6 |
| Flow matching, 3 steps | 18.11 | 0.615 | 0.419 | 0.252 | 54.9 |
| Normalizing flow, one sample | 15.77 | 0.410 | 0.577 | 0.316 | 120.0 |
| Normalizing flow, 256-pixel patches | 15.85 | 0.423 | 0.568 | 0.320 | 124.9 |

These test metrics average over the same 24 Kodak images; stochastic-model KID uses eight samples per image. The normalizing flow's eight-sample mean reaches 17.69 dB, 1.92 dB above its single-sample PSNR and 0.68 dB above midpoint fill, but remains below the residual U-Net. Its mean diversity is 23.94 intensity levels, much higher than diffusion/flow matching at about 6.1. Against residual U-Net, it wins per-image PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on 0/24 images, and DISTS on 7/24. Its KID (0.120) is worse than the random-fill set (0.119) and much worse than flow matching (0.055). MSB consistency is exact. The inspected sample strips show noisy texture and visible independent-patch boundaries.

### 256-pixel patch ablation

Both checkpoints use the same 40k steps, four scales, four blocks per scale, train/validation sets and seed. Only patch/crop size and batch size changed (128/16 to 256/4).

| Patch size | PSNR ↑ | PSNR avg ↑ | SSIM ↑ | LPIPS ↓ | DISTS ↓ | KID × 10³ ↓ | Diversity | Time/image |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 128 | 15.77 | 17.69 | 0.410 | 0.577 | **0.316** | **120.0** | 23.94 | 18 ms |
| 256 | **15.85** | **17.70** | **0.423** | **0.568** | 0.320 | 124.9 | 22.98 | **14 ms** |

The 0.08 dB single-sample PSNR and 0.02 dB sample-mean PSNR gains are negligible for this 24-image test. SSIM/LPIPS improve slightly, while DISTS/KID worsen slightly; the set-level KID is near the 128-patch value within its reported subset spread. Against residual U-Net, the 256 model wins PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on 0/24 images and DISTS on 6/24. Visually, 256 reduces the obvious patch grid in the inspected scene but still produces noisy, soft structure. It also runs about 22% faster per image because it uses fewer, larger patches. This is not enough to justify another NF architecture run.

**Interpretation:** the code, likelihood, inverse sampling, and MSB preservation function, but this conditional Glow configuration is not a competitive perceptual decoder. Keep both checkpoints/results as a negative baseline. The dequantized NLL remains a density-model metric, not achieved lossless bpp. Do not build the planned NF+FM+diffusion hybrid on this result.

### k=2 pilot results (completed; followed by completed k=3 run)

The Kodak evaluation uses the same 24 images at k=1 and k=2. Mean PSNR (dB) improved across every tested method:

| Method | k=1 | k=2 | Change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Midpoint fill | 17.01 | 22.63 | +5.62 |
| CNN | 18.04 | 25.13 | +7.09 |
| U-Net | 18.55 | 25.90 | +7.35 |
| Residual U-Net | 18.60 | 26.14 | +7.54 |
| Diffusion (3 steps) | 17.75 | 25.63 | +7.88 |
| Flow matching (3 steps) | 18.11 | 25.60 | +7.50 |

For the matched 3-step generative evaluations, DISTS and patch KID also improved from k=1 to k=2:

| Model | k=1 PSNR / DISTS / KID | k=2 PSNR / DISTS / KID |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Diffusion | 17.75 / 0.2567 / 0.0546 | 25.63 / 0.1292 / 0.0261 |
| Flow matching | 18.11 / 0.2519 / 0.0549 | 25.60 / 0.1302 / 0.0253 |

PSNR is for one sample; DISTS is lower-is-better, and KID is computed over eight generated samples per image. At k=2, residual U-Net has the best single-sample PSNR (26.14 dB), while diffusion and flow matching have better perceptual scores (DISTS 0.129-0.130 vs 0.155; KID 0.025-0.026 vs 0.042). The mean of eight generative samples reaches about 26.24 dB, slightly above residual U-Net, at the cost of eight draws. Residual-output models preserve the transmitted planes exactly; direct CNN/U-Net outputs have small MSB-consistency errors.

There is no single best step count across metrics or k. At k=1, one step gives the highest single-sample PSNR, two steps the lowest KID, and five steps the lowest DISTS. At k=2, one step gives the highest single-sample PSNR, two steps the best PSNR of the eight-sample mean (about 26.24-26.25 dB), and three steps the lowest DISTS/KID. Moving from two to three steps costs about 0.23 dB in single-sample PSNR but improves DISTS/KID; beyond three steps those perceptual scores worsen while diversity rises. In this evaluator, k=2 diffusion/flow matching takes about 20, 41, 60, 99, and 960 ms per image at 1, 2, 3, 5, and 50 steps, respectively, versus about 6 ms for residual U-Net. In the inspected Kodak example, k=2 also preserves the broad window layout better than k=1, though fine detail is still smoothed or synthesized. These are pilot results on 24 Kodak images, not evidence of generalization. The raw input cost doubles from 1 to 2 bits/pixel; actual coded bitrate has not yet been measured.

This pilot led to the completed k=3 comparison below. The normalizing-flow track was evaluated separately and retained as a negative k=1 baseline; it was not extended to k=2 or k=3.

At k=3 the generative models nearly match the residual U-Net in PSNR, while their DISTS and KID are substantially better. Their eight-sample mean reaches 32.37 dB for diffusion and 32.34 dB for flow matching, above the residual U-Net, with diversity only 2.35-2.42 intensity levels. Direct CNN/U-Net still violate the transmitted planes slightly; residual and generative models remain exact.

The k=3 step sweep confirms the same perception-distortion pattern as k=1 and k=2: one step gives the highest PSNR and lowest latency, while three-to-five steps are the perceptual operating region. At three steps, diffusion is marginally ahead of flow matching (31.58 vs 31.51 dB, DISTS 0.0717 vs 0.0719, KID 8.6 vs 9.0 × 10^-3); the difference is small relative to the 24-image benchmark. No additional k=3 architecture training is justified.

Raw input cost is now 1, 2, or 3 bits/pixel before entropy coding. The reconstruction experiments show a strong rate-quality curve, but they do not establish the actual compressed rate. **Next:** begin Milestone 8: measure per-plane entropy/context entropy, implement lossless coding for the transmitted planes, and report actual bpp alongside PSNR, DISTS, KID, and decoding time. Defer the NF and hybrid tracks unless the compression study creates a likelihood-specific question.

## 25. Your biggest technical risks

There are several.

### Risk 1 — One MSB may simply be too little information

This is the most fundamental risk.

Your model could produce images that are visually plausible but poorly correlated with the original.

That's not necessarily a failure—but you need to characterize it.

---

### Risk 2 — Hallucination

Diffusion/flow models can create realistic details that **did not exist in the original**.

That's acceptable for perceptual compression, but not for applications requiring fidelity.

You need to clearly define the application.

---

### Risk 3 — Dataset bias

A model trained on faces might reconstruct plausible faces extremely well but fail on arbitrary images.

Therefore eventually test cross-domain.

---

### Risk 4 — Computation

Full-resolution diffusion can be extremely expensive.

Start at:

$$
64\times64
$$

or

$$
128\times128.
$$

Then move to:

$$
256\times256.
$$

Do not start at 1024×1024.

---

### Risk 5 — Hybrid complexity

A model containing:

```text
NF + FM + diffusion
```

may be difficult to train and may not outperform a simpler model.

Your research should allow the conclusion:

> "Flow matching alone provides the best tradeoff."

That is scientifically much better than forcing the hybrid to win.

## 26. The research hypothesis I recommend

I would write the central hypothesis as:

> **An MSB bitplane can serve as an extremely low-rate structural representation of an image, while a conditional generative prior can reconstruct the missing lower-order intensity information, enabling an ultra-low-bitrate perceptual image codec.**

Then the second hypothesis:

> **Flow-based generative reconstruction can provide a better quality–complexity tradeoff than conventional diffusion for MSB-conditioned image reconstruction.**

And the third:

> **Progressively transmitting additional MSB planes provides a controllable rate–distortion–perception tradeoff.**

These are testable hypotheses rather than assumptions.
